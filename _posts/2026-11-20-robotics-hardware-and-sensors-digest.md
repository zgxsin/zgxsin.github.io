The first issue covers November 10–20. A research team showed how to measure shear across a thin optical array, Melexis packaged three-axis force sensing into a fingertip, and Hesai reported higher shipments of robotics lidar and actuation modules.

## Markets

**November 10 · Hesai reports higher lidar and actuator shipments.** In its unaudited third-quarter results, Hesai reported shipping 91,618 robotics lidar units, up 51.1% year over year, and 23,405 robotic actuation modules, up 96.8% from the previous quarter. The company reports robotics lidar separately from driver-assistance lidar, so the robotics figure is the one to track for changes in its robotics sensor business. [Hesai third-quarter results](https://hesai.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hesai-group-reports-third-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial)

## Academia

**November 17 · LuxTac measures shear at individual points on an optical array.** The preprint describes a flexible array about 3.5 mm thick. A gel cartridge with colored walls encodes sideways deformation in the light returned to each tactile element, allowing the array to estimate normal force and shear without a camera. The authors report a median shear-direction error of 4.8° in their bench tests. They also report that adding pressure readings improved peg-in-hole performance over their vision-only baseline. [LuxTac preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23575)

## Industry

**November 12 · Melexis introduces a three-axis tactile fingertip.** The MLX90901 combines a magnetic sensor, elastomer, mechanics, and readout electronics in one module. Melexis specifies a 15 N normal-force range and a 4 N shear-force range, and says its DVK90901 evaluation kit is available. Measurement along the two shear axes gives a grasp controller a way to observe changes in lateral loading while it holds an object. [Melexis product announcement](https://www.melexis.com/en/news/2026/12nov2026-tactile-fingertip-module-sense-of-touch-humanoid-robots)
